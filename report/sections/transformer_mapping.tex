\section{From Tests to Transformers}\label{sec:transformer}

The BLB test binaries are not standalone benchmarks --- each exercises a specific cryptographic primitive that corresponds to a layer in private Transformer inference.
This section explains how the test suite maps onto the BERT architecture and what a full private inference pipeline looks like.

\subsection{Private Transformer Inference Pipeline}

A Transformer encoder block (as in BERT~\cite{xu2025breaking}) consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Multi-Head Self-Attention}: $Q, K, V$ projections (linear), attention scores ($QK^T$), softmax, weighted sum, output projection (linear).
  \item \textbf{Layer Normalization}: mean, variance, normalize, affine transform.
  \item \textbf{Feed-Forward Network (FFN)}: linear $\to$ GeLU/SiLU $\to$ linear.
  \item \textbf{Layer Normalization} (again).
\end{enumerate}

In BLB's hybrid CKKS+MPC model, each operation is assigned to the most efficient cryptographic backend:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Mapping Transformer operations to BLB cryptographic primitives}
\label{tab:transformer-mapping}
\begin{tabular}{p{4cm}p{3.5cm}p{2.5cm}p{3cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Transformer Op} & \textbf{BLB Primitive} & \textbf{Backend} & \textbf{Test Binary} \\
\midrule
$Q, K, V$ projections        & CirLinearNest      & HE (CKKS)   & \texttt{test\_cir\_linear} \\
Attention $QK^T$              & LinearNest (matmul) & HE (CKKS)  & \texttt{test\_matmul} \\
Attention softmax             & FixPoint comparison  & MPC (OT)   & \texttt{test\_fixpoint} \\
Attention $\text{softmax} \cdot V$       & LinearNest (matmul) & HE (CKKS) & \texttt{test\_matmul} \\
Attention output projection   & CirLinearNest      & HE (CKKS)   & \texttt{test\_cir\_linear} \\
LayerNorm (mean, var, scale)  & FixPoint truncate/round & MPC (OT) & \texttt{test\_fixpoint} \\
FFN first linear              & CirLinearNest      & HE (CKKS)   & \texttt{test\_cir\_linear} \\
FFN activation (GeLU)         & ElementWiseMul + FixPoint & HE+MPC & \texttt{test\_linear\_operator} \\
FFN second linear             & CirLinearNest      & HE (CKKS)   & \texttt{test\_cir\_linear} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The key insight from BLB~\cite{xu2025breaking} is that \textbf{linear layers use HE} (ciphertext rotations on packed polynomials, no communication rounds) while \textbf{nonlinear operations use MPC} (OT-based comparison requires communication but avoids expensive HE bootstrapping).
This hybrid approach breaks the ``layer barrier'' --- previous systems required HE bootstrapping or MPC round-trips for every layer, but BLB keeps data in the HE domain across multiple linear layers, only switching to MPC for nonlinearities.

\subsection{Test-to-BERT Mapping}

\subsubsection{test\_ntt: Polynomial Foundation}

Every CKKS operation ultimately reduces to NTT (Number Theoretic Transform) on polynomials in the ring $\mathbb{Z}_Q[X]/(X^N+1)$.
When SEAL evaluates a ciphertext rotation or multiplication, it performs forward NTT, element-wise multiply in the NTT domain, and inverse NTT.
Intel HEXL accelerates this with AVX-512 SIMD intrinsics.

\textbf{Role in Transformer}: \texttt{test\_ntt} validates the foundation that \emph{every other HE operation} builds on.
A single BERT-base attention layer with $H{=}768$ involves hundreds of NTT operations for the rotation-based matrix multiplication.

\subsubsection{test\_linear\_operator: HE Element-Wise Computation}

Tests CKKS element-wise operations: $\mathbf{x}^2$ and $\mathbf{x} \odot \mathbf{y}$ on 8192-slot ciphertexts with values in $[-5, 5]$.

\textbf{Role in Transformer}: Element-wise multiply is the primitive for:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{GeLU/SiLU activation}: The polynomial approximation $\text{GeLU}(x) \approx c_0 x^4 + c_1 x^3 + c_2 x^2 + c_3 x + c_4$ requires computing $x^2$ and $x^4 = (x^2)^2$ via ciphertext-ciphertext multiplication.
  BLB's GeLU uses coefficients: $c_0 {=} 0.0208$, $c_1 {=} {-}0.1835$, $c_2 {=} 0.5411$, $c_3 {=} {-}0.0380$, $c_4 {=} 0.0016$.
  \item \textbf{Attention score scaling}: The $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_h}}$ scaling factor applied to $QK^T$ scores.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{test\_matmul: Attention Score Computation}

Tests HE matrix multiplication with dimensions $128 \times 64 \times 64$ using the diagonal packing method with BSGS optimization.

\textbf{Role in Transformer}: This directly corresponds to:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Attention scores}: $\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_h}})V$. The $QK^T$ computation is a $(s \times d_h) \times (d_h \times s)$ matmul where $s$ is sequence length.
        For BERT-tiny: $(128 \times 64) \times (64 \times 128)$.
  \item \textbf{Weighted value sum}: The $\text{softmax}(\cdot) \times V$ computation is a $(s \times s) \times (s \times d_h)$ matmul.
\end{itemize}

The test dimensions ($128 \times 64 \times 64$) match BERT-tiny/mini's head dimensions ($d_h {=} 64$) with sequence length 128.

The BSGS optimization reduces rotation count from $O(d)$ to $O(2\sqrt{d})$.
For $d{=}64$: na\"ive requires 64 rotations, BSGS requires $2 \times 8 = 16$ rotations --- a $4\times$ reduction.

\subsubsection{test\_Conv \& test\_cir\_conv: Dense vs.\ PrivCirNet}

\texttt{test\_Conv} tests standard dense convolution; \texttt{test\_cir\_conv} tests the PrivCirNet block-circulant variant.
While convolution is primarily a CNN operation, the underlying HE rotation machinery is shared with linear layers.

\textbf{Role in Transformer}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Vision Transformer (ViT)}: The patch embedding layer is a convolution with kernel size = patch size.
  \item \textbf{Convolution-based attention}: Some efficient Transformer variants use depthwise convolution instead of attention for local feature extraction.
  \item \textbf{Rotation benchmark}: \texttt{test\_Conv} is the most rotation-heavy test and establishes the ceiling for HE rotation overhead. The $3.5\times$ speedup of \texttt{test\_cir\_conv} demonstrates the rotation savings from circulant structure.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{test\_cir\_linear: PrivCirNet Linear Layers}

Tests CirLinearNest with dimensions $64 \times 256 \times 256$ and block sizes from 1 to 64.

\textbf{Role in Transformer}: This is the \textbf{most critical test for BERT inference} --- it directly implements:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{$Q, K, V$ projections}: Each attention head projects from $H$ to $d_h$. For BERT-tiny: $128 \to 64$.
        With PrivCirNet, the weight matrix $W_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times d_h}$ is constrained to block-circulant structure, reducing HE rotations.
  \item \textbf{Output projection}: Concatenated attention heads projected back to $H$.
  \item \textbf{FFN layers}: The two linear layers in the FFN block ($H \to 4H$ and $4H \to H$).
        For BERT-tiny: $128 \to 512$ and $512 \to 128$.
\end{itemize}

The block-circulant structure means each block of the weight matrix is a circulant matrix defined by its first row.
Multiplication becomes polynomial multiplication in the NTT domain:
\[
\mathbf{y}_{\text{block}} = \text{INTT}(\text{NTT}(\mathbf{w}_{\text{row}_0}) \odot \text{NTT}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{block}}))
\]

The test dimension ($256 \times 256$) matches BERT-mini's hidden dimension.
Scaling to BERT-base ($768 \times 768$) increases the number of blocks but not the per-block cost.

\subsubsection{test\_fixpoint: Softmax, LayerNorm, and Quantization}

Tests IKNP OT-based fixed-point operations: \texttt{less\_than\_constant}, comparison, truncation, ring-field conversion.

\textbf{Role in Transformer}: These primitives implement every nonlinear operation:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Softmax}: $\text{softmax}(x_i) = \frac{e^{x_i}}{\sum_j e^{x_j}}$.
        BLB approximates $e^x$ via $(1 + x/64)^{64}$ (repeated squaring in fixed-point).
        The normalization requires secure comparison (who has the max?) and secure division (truncation).
  \item \textbf{LayerNorm}: $\text{LN}(x) = \gamma \cdot \frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}} + \beta$.
        Computing $\mu$ (mean) and $\sigma^2$ (variance) requires secure accumulation and division (truncation).
        The $1/\sqrt{\sigma^2}$ term uses Newton's method with secure comparison for convergence.
  \item \textbf{GeLU transition}: Before computing $\text{GeLU}(x)$ via polynomial approximation in the HE domain, the input must be converted from MPC ring representation to HE-compatible format (\texttt{Ring2Field} / \texttt{Field2Ring}).
  \item \textbf{Requantization}: After each linear layer, the output bitwidth grows. \texttt{secure\_round} and \texttt{truncate\_reduce} bring it back to the target precision (e.g., 16-bit), preventing overflow in subsequent layers.
\end{itemize}

The 16-bit fixed-point representation ($s{=}12$ fractional bits) provides $2^{-12} \approx 0.00024$ resolution, sufficient for BERT inference where activations are typically in $[-4, 4]$.

\textbf{Important caveats}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item These are upper-bound estimates: the test parameters do not exactly match BERT-tiny dimensions, and the CirLinearNest test uses $256 \times 256$ (larger than BERT-tiny's $128 \times 128$).
  \item SEAL key generation is a one-time cost amortized across all layers. The per-block estimate double-counts key generation ($\sim$1.2\,s per HE test).
  \item With key caching and amortized setup, the actual per-block time would be lower.
  \item A GPU-accelerated build (PhantomFHE) would significantly reduce HE operation time.
\end{itemize}

The scaling is approximately:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Linear in $L$} (number of layers): each encoder block is independent.
  \item \textbf{Quadratic in $H$} (hidden dimension): CirLinearNest operates on $H \times H$ matrices. Doubling $H$ roughly quadruples the per-layer cost because the number of circulant blocks increases as $H^2 / b^2$.
  \item \textbf{Linear in $s$} (sequence length): attention is $O(s^2 d_h)$ but the BLB matmul packs $s$ into CKKS slots, so the HE cost scales linearly with $s$ up to the slot capacity ($N/2 = 4096$ for $N{=}8192$).
\end{itemize}

For practical deployment, the BLB paper~\cite{xu2025breaking} targets BERT-tiny and BERT-mini on GPU, where PhantomFHE reduces HE operation time by 10--50$\times$ compared to CPU SEAL.
